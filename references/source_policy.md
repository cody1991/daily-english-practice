# Source Policy

Build a stable source pool, then vary topic and difficulty inside it.

## Preferred Pools

- Authentic everyday speech: street interviews, short interviews, and conversational videos with English subtitles.
- Workplace communication: official podcast pages with transcripts, especially material about meetings, feedback, clarification, disagreement, decisions, and collaboration.
- Scaffolded fallback: English-learning audio with transcript when the learner marks recent material as too difficult or has limited time.
- Netherlands context: English-language reporting or explainers about local life, used as a topic bridge rather than the main listening source.

## Candidate Checks

Choose a candidate only when it has all of the following:

1. A canonical official URL.
2. A 3-8 minute usable segment with exact start and end timestamps. A short standalone source still records `00:00–end`.
3. English subtitles or a transcript accessible to the learner.
4. A clear conversational payoff: an expression, interaction move, or retellable idea.
5. No recent URL, source-family, or scenario collision in `.learning/state.json`.

## Avoid

- Uncaptioned clips, reaction compilations, and content with poor audio.
- Long academic talks used without a short, precisely timestamped segment.
- Teaching content as the only source type; use it to lower difficulty, not to replace real voices.
- Song lyrics as daily core material. Use music occasionally for enjoyment or rhythm only.
